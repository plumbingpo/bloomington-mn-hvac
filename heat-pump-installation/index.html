Many Bloomington, MN homeowners replacing an aging furnace and AC are switching to a single heat pump instead, and the reason is efficiency. A heat pump does not burn fuel or heat a resistance coil; it moves heat. In cooling mode it carries heat out of the house, and in heating mode a reversing valve sends the refrigerant the other way so the compressor gathers heat from outdoor air and releases it indoors. That lets it deliver two to three times more heat than the electricity it consumes. Modern cold-climate equipment with inverter-driven compressors keeps working well below freezing, which the older units behind the technology's old reputation simply could not do.

Getting that performance takes design work up front, and this is where Bloomington HVAC earns the job. Sizing a heat pump means reconciling two loads: the summer cooling demand and the winter heating demand, and finding the balance point where outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, weigh single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed inverter options against your climate and ducts, and design the backup heat correctly, whether that is electric strips staged to assist below the balance point or a dual-fuel pairing with a gas furnace that takes over in the deepest cold. We also verify the electrical service and the duct system, because heat pumps deliver their heat at lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need real airflow to feel right.

Installation follows the same discipline as every Bloomington HVAC system: line set pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, charge weighed in and confirmed with superheat and subcooling, the outdoor unit elevated on a stand where winter defrost drainage demands it, condensate handled properly, and the thermostat configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost behave the way the engineering intended. Then we commission in both modes, heating and cooling, and record the readings. Why Hire a Professional for Heat Pump Installation?

Since a heat pump runs in both seasons, installation mistakes cost you twice. An incorrect charge cuts cooling efficiency in summer and heating capacity in winter. Skip the balance point calculation and electric backup strips may end up heating the house at several times the operating cost, leaving the homeowner puzzled about why the bills never dropped. Leave the thermostat on factory defaults and it can call for backup heat the compressor could have handled, or interfere with defrost. Set the outdoor unit flat on the ground in a real winter and it sits in refrozen defrost water until the coil turns to ice.

Our experienced technicians close off each of those failure points by design. We calculate both seasonal loads, choose equipment for its real capacity curve at your local winter design temperature, and configure backup heat staging and lockouts so the strips or furnace only run when they should. The outdoor unit is elevated to drain its defrost water, and the charge is weighed in and verified. At startup we commission heating and cooling separately, confirm the reversing valve, and watch a defrost cycle finish. With federally regulated refrigerant work and inverter electronics involved, precision matters, and it is the installation that decides whether a heat pump is a delight or a disappointment.

Maintenance Tips for Heat Pump Installation

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Watch the first winter bill; a sudden spike usually means backup heat is overworking
  • A little winter frost is normal; a unit encased in ice is not, so give us a call
  • Keep a regular filter schedule so the system has enough air in summer and winter
  • Let the system run steady; big setbacks trigger expensive backup heat on recovery
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow drifts, leaves, and shrubs year-round
  • Plan two tune-ups a year so the system is verified in both heating and cooling
  • Clean the outdoor coil each spring for a strong start to summer
