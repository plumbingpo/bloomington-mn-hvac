Where do most HVAC installations go wrong? Usually before the first wrench turns, when someone matches the new tonnage to the old nameplate and skips the math. At Bloomington HVAC, the math comes first. We build a load calculation for your Bloomington, MN home from its square footage, insulation levels, windows, orientation, and air leakage, because a system sized by habit rather than by measurement is wrong from day one. From there the job becomes three connected pieces of work: equipment matched to the load, ductwork able to deliver what that equipment produces, and a commissioning process that proves the result with real readings instead of on paper.

Equipment selection follows the load, and matching matters more than most homeowners are told. The indoor coil, outdoor unit, and blower are rated as matched combinations, and mismatched pairings surrender the SEER2 and AFUE numbers on the sticker. We walk you through the real trade-offs between single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed equipment, blower options, and pairings with a furnace, air handler, or heat pump, in plain language and without pressure. Then the ductwork gets the attention it almost never receives: our experienced technicians measure static pressure, hunt down leaky and undersized runs, and correct the chokepoints while the system is open, because a duct system that fights the equipment defeats it.

The install itself is a checklist, not a guess: line sets pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, charge weighed in and confirmed by superheat and subcooling, gas connections leak-tested, venting run to manufacturer spec, condensate trapped and protected by a float switch, and the thermostat configured for the equipment's actual staging. We finish with full commissioning, recording temperature split, temperature rise, amp draws, and static pressure, then walk you through everything before we haul the old equipment away. The Benefits of Professional HVAC Installation

An HVAC system is the largest machine in most homes, and installation is where its entire service life gets decided. The failure modes are invisible at handoff: a vacuum never verified means moisture breeding acid in the refrigerant loop, a charge set by feel means efficiency quietly surrendered, mismatched coil-and-condenser pairings mean the rated numbers were never achievable, and duct restrictions mean the blower spends fifteen years straining against static pressure it was never designed for. Refrigerant work is federally regulated and gas and venting mistakes are safety problems, not just performance problems.

Professional installation is a chain of verifications. Our experienced technicians size from a load calculation, install matched combinations, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, weigh the charge and prove it with superheat and subcooling, leak-test the gas side, verify draft, and set blower speeds to hold the rated temperature rise. Then we commission with recorded numbers, which become the baseline every future service visit can be checked against. That paper trail of measurements is the difference between a system that delivers its rated efficiency for fifteen years and one that merely runs, and it is standard on every installation we do.

Maintenance Tips for HVAC Installation

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Keep supply and return vents open to preserve the airflow balance set at commissioning
  • Deal with minor rattles and comfort changes before they grow into repairs
  • Flush the drain line before summer to keep algae from clogging it
  • Keep a spring and fall maintenance visit on the calendar every year
  • Save your commissioning report and share it with whoever services the system
  • Start a regular filter schedule right away, since steady airflow protects the coil and the heat exchanger
  • Rinse the outdoor coil and keep plants trimmed back so the condenser can breathe
